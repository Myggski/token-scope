      Help

      Prices and models

      Where prices come from, how sorting works and how to compare models

      Where do prices come from?

      TokenScope gets prices from OpenRouter, which collects pricing information from AI providers and updates it as prices change.

      These are pay-per-token prices. If you use a fixed-price subscription, your plan's usage limits may matter more than these prices.

      How does Cheapest work?

      Sorts models by estimated cost, counting three tokens read for every token written. This is a rough estimate of the balance between reading and writing during coding work.

      How does Best for the buck work?

      Balances price and release date equally. Newer models and cheaper models rank higher.

      Newer models aren't always better, so use this ranking as a starting point, not a guarantee.

      Why is there no quality score?

      There isn't a free quality score that covers most of these models. DeepSWE's coding scores are outdated and cover only a few models. OpenRouter has scores for only a small selection, too.

      Using these scores would favor models that happen to have been tested. The best way to judge a model is to try it on your own work.

      What are favorites for?

      Star the models you use regularly. The Favorites filter shows only those models, and your AI uses them when estimating a task unless you choose other models.

      What does the warning under a model mean?

      The provider plans to shut down the model on the date shown. Avoid relying on it for work that needs to continue after that date.

      Plans

      Task estimates, unexpected costs and real spending

      How do I get an estimate?

      Before starting a task, ask your AI to plan it with TokenScope. For example:

      "Plan this first, estimate the cost with TokenScope using Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and wait for my go-ahead."

      Your AI plans the work without making changes, estimates the cost for each model and waits for your approval.

      If you don't name any models, TokenScope uses your favorites. You can change the models later in the plan's Details.

      How are plans estimated?

      Your AI describes the work it expects to do, including how many steps it might take, how much text it will read and how much it will write.

      TokenScope uses that information to estimate the cost for each model.

      What does the cost range mean?

      The low end assumes the task goes smoothly, without needing fixes. The high end allows for 50% more steps.

      Use the high end when setting your budget. Tasks that require more fixes can cost more than the estimate.

      What are cache misses?

      AI tools can reuse recently read conversation text at a lower price. If that text can no longer be reused, the AI has to pay the full price to read it again. This is called a cache miss.

      It can happen after you've been away for a few minutes, after a long build, when you switch models or when you start a new conversation. These extra reading costs can add up.

      Quick back-and-forth with your AI doesn't cause cache misses by itself.

      How are real costs measured?

      TokenScope measures costs automatically as you work. A plan's real cost counts everything from the estimate until your AI says the task is done, or until another plan starts in the same conversation.

      Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and Zed report exact token counts, so their costs are exact. Cursor costs are approximate because it doesn't report exact token usage, and they're only measured when Cursor is turned on under Track AI spending.

      Other tools only get estimates.

      Is there a shortcut for estimates?

      Some AI tools offer a shortcut for asking TokenScope to estimate a task.

      • Claude Code: Type /mcp__tokenscope__estimate followed by your task.
      • Cursor: Type /estimate.
      • Claude Desktop: Open the + menu, select TokenScope, then choose Plan and estimate.

      You can also ask in your own words. That works across all supported tools.

      Do estimates get better over time?

      Yes. Once two plans have real costs, TokenScope starts adjusting new estimates based on how far previous estimates were from the real costs.

      Spending

      Tracked conversations, cost breakdowns and ways to save

      What does Spending show?

      Every conversation from the AI tools you've chosen to track, whether you planned it or not.

      You can see what each conversation cost, which models it used and what contributed to the cost.

      Search finds conversations by name, tool, project, model or maker (like Anthropic or OpenAI), in any word order. The totals follow the search.

      What would a conversation have cost on another model?

      Open a conversation's Details. Under On other models, TokenScope prices the same tokens on your favorite models, or on models you add there, and shows how much cheaper or more expensive each would have been.

      It's a rough comparison: another model would also work differently and take a different number of steps.

      How do I get suggestions?

      Ask your AI something like:

      "What made my AI work expensive this week, and how can I spend less?"

      Your AI can use your spending data to answer once TokenScope is connected to your AI tool under Connect your AI tool.

      How do I set it up?

      Turn on the tools you want to track under Track AI spending.

      Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and Zed need no extra setup. TokenScope reads their existing usage records.

      For Cursor, turning on tracking adds a small hook to Cursor's settings. Turning it off removes that hook. Cursor applies the change automatically, so no restart is needed.

      If your Cursor spending doesn't appear after you've used it, restart Cursor once.

      How accurate are the costs?

      Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode report exact token counts, so their costs are exact.

      Zed's costs are exact too, from Zed's own token counts, but Zed saves only one total per conversation. So Spending can't show what drove a Zed conversation's cost, and a plan made in a Zed chat costs the whole chat. If you switch models during a chat, the whole chat is priced as the last model.

      Cursor doesn't report exact token counts. TokenScope works out its costs from the size of each step and from Cursor's own count of how big each chat was, tuned against Cursor's usage page. These costs are marked with ≈ and are usually within about 15% per conversation. Cursor's usage page has the exact amounts.

      Estimates learn from your finished plans, per tool. After 2 finished plans in a tool, its new estimates are corrected by how far the earlier ones were off. Tools differ a lot: Cursor runs many actions in one request, so the same work costs less there. Until a tool has 2 plans of its own, its estimates use the correction from plans with an exact cost.

      Costs use today's prices, except for older conversations a tool no longer keeps, which keep the prices from when TokenScope saved them.

      Why is a conversation missing?

      Spending only shows the tools turned on under Track AI spending, and only work done on this computer. Claude Code in the cloud or in a browser, Claude Desktop and ChatGPT keep no records here. Cursor conversations show up from when you turned Cursor on. Tools inside WSL are read only while WSL is running. And Spending only goes back as far as Keep spending history for.

      What does the Cursor hook save?

      It saves usage details: how much text each step read and wrote, how many characters an edit changed, which model was used, when it happened, and whether a step was a build or a test.

      It also saves a scrambled identifier for your Cursor account, not your email address.

      It never saves your code, prompts, commands or the AI's replies.

      TokenScope also reads each chat's name and size from Cursor's own data on your computer, without changing it. It reads nothing else from there.

      Your data and privacy

      What TokenScope saves, what it reads and what happens when you move or uninstall it

      What's saved?

      Everything is stored in the folder shown under Your data.

      • tokenscope-data.json stores your plans, favorites and settings. Copy this file to back them up.
      • The history folder stores usage recorded by the Cursor hook and summaries of conversations shown in Spending. These summaries contain costs, models and cost breakdowns, not the conversations themselves.
      • The mcp folder holds the copy of TokenScope your AI tools run. It's refreshed every time TokenScope starts, so it always matches the installed version.
      • Other files are used by TokenScope to run the app.

      TokenScope saves conversation summaries so your spending history can last longer than the original records. For example, Claude Code deletes its own records after 30 days.

      What does TokenScope read?

      TokenScope reads the usage records already saved by Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and Zed, but only for the tools you've turned on under Track AI spending.

      It only reads those records and never changes them. It saves summaries of the conversations for Spending instead.

      Claude Code's records are normally stored in . Codex's records are normally stored in .

      If you run these tools inside WSL, TokenScope can also read their records there while WSL is running.

      Does anything leave my computer?

      TokenScope sends requests to OpenRouter to get model prices, and checks GitHub for a new version of TokenScope. These requests don't include information about you or your work.

      When a new version is ready, TokenScope asks before installing it. If you choose Later, it installs the next time you close TokenScope.

      Does TokenScope change anything outside its folder?

      Only Cursor's hook, when you turn on Cursor tracking. After an update, TokenScope keeps its own hook up to date. It never adds itself to your AI tools. You do that under Connect your AI tool.

      What does "data mismatch" mean?

      A source, such as OpenRouter or Cursor, has provided data that TokenScope doesn't recognize. This can happen when the source changes its data format.

      The warning tells you which source is affected. Costs from that source may be incorrect until TokenScope is updated.

      What's remembered between launches?

      TokenScope remembers your favorites, filters, sorting, token budget, search and theme.

      It always opens on Models, with sections collapsed.

      To reset these settings, use Reset settings under Your data.

      What if I move TokenScope?

      Your AI tools and the Cursor hook find TokenScope through its folder location. Moving it can break those connections, but your saved data isn't affected.

      After moving TokenScope, reconnect your AI tools under Connect your AI tool. If you use Cursor tracking, turn it off and on again under Track AI spending.

      What if I uninstall TokenScope?

      Turn off Cursor tracking first so TokenScope removes the Cursor hook.

      Your data folder stays on your computer after you uninstall the app.
